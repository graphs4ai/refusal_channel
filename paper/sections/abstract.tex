\begin{abstract}
Multi-turn sycophancy evaluations can present the evaluated model with a
preconstructed dialogue history whose assistant turns it did not generate. We
audit one such scaffold in a controlled multiple-choice setting with eight
open-weight instruction-tuned models. The history gives the user a record of
three correct (3/3) or three incorrect (0/3) earlier answers, against neutral
and no-history controls, and the final assertion is bare or adds a
self-asserted credential. Four models return no option in
$\naPctQwS$--$\naPctOlm\%$ of pressured records, and $\pctNonAnswerAckPress\%$
of these non-answers are verbatim copies of the prefilled acknowledgement
``\texttt{Noted.}''. Non-answers persist when the final assertion is omitted,
shift with small system-prompt edits, and almost vanish when the assistant
turns are removed or self-generated. Because their rate depends on the
condition, endorsement over all records and endorsement among parsed answers
give history contrasts of opposite sign for two models; without prefilled
acknowledgements, endorsement is lower after a 0/3 record in most models. With
no history, a matched no-pressure control departs from the baseline answer
$\nullShareMin$--$\nullShareMax\%$ as often as the pressured record. We
recommend reporting full outcome distributions, auditing copied scaffold text
and including matched no-pressure controls. Code, prompts and generations are
released.
\end{abstract}
