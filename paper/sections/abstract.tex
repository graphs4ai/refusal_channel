\begin{abstract}
Multi-turn sycophancy evaluations often build the dialogue history from
pre-filled assistant turns and then score whether the model endorses a user's
wrong answer. We show that this construction can create a response category
that the score does not anticipate. Across eight open-weight instruction-tuned
models, we vary the user's demonstrated track record (3/3 vs.\ 0/3 verified
correct claims), an unverifiable credential, and a length-matched neutral
history, and we report four outcomes: correct, endorsement of the suggested
option, another wrong option, and non-answer. Four models frequently return
no option (in shares of $\naQwS$ to $\naOlm$ of pressured records), and
$\pctNonAnswerAck\%$ of all non-answers reproduce the pre-filled
acknowledgement verbatim. Non-answers also occur without any user assertion
when the same scaffold is kept, and they fall to zero in all four models when
the assistant turns are removed or replaced by the model's own fixed replies.
Because non-answers depend on the manipulated history, the two common scoring
conventions, counting them as non-endorsement or excluding them, can reverse
the sign of a history effect within one model. With self-generated turns,
endorsement after a 0/3 record is lower than after a 3/3 record in
$\nSelfEndNeg$ of the eight models. A matched no-pressure control accounts for
$\nullShareMin$--$\nullShareMax\%$ of the raw answer-change rate. Code,
prompts and all generations are released.
\end{abstract}
