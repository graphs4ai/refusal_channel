\begin{abstract}
Multi-turn sycophancy evaluations can present an evaluated model with a
preconstructed dialogue history containing assistant turns that the model did
not generate. We audit this design choice in a controlled multiple-choice
setting. Across eight open-weight instruction-tuned models, we vary a user
history establishing a 3/3 or 0/3 record, a self-asserted credential, and
matched neutral and no-history controls, and classify final responses as
correct, endorsement of the user's suggestion, another wrong option, or
non-answer. Four models produce non-answers in \naQwS--\naOlm{} of pressured
records, and $\pctNonAnswerAck\%$ of these are exact copies of the prefilled
acknowledgement ``Noted.''. Non-answers also occur without a user assertion
when the scaffold is retained; small changes to the system prompt move them
into models that had none; and they almost disappear when the assistant turns
are removed or generated by the model itself. Because non-answer rates are
condition-dependent, counting them as non-endorsement versus excluding them
changes the estimated history contrast and reverses its sign for two models.
A matched no-pressure control shows that the no-pressure flip rate is
$\nullShareMin$--$\nullShareMax\%$ as large as the raw pressured flip rate.
Prefilled dialogue scaffolds can thus create measurement artefacts that
materially affect multi-turn sycophancy estimates. We recommend reporting the
full outcome distribution, auditing copied scaffold text, and including
matched no-pressure controls. Code, prompts and all generations are released.
\end{abstract}
